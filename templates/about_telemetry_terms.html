Telemetry terms

This is a highly experimental feature which is not ready for wide adoption. It simply won't scale to a large audience, and I might struggle to keep the site running smoothly if user numbers grow. And to be honest, I do consider the competitive element too: this tool aims to help my daughter. However, my goal is to help her grow, rather than make her faster than others through technology. Healthy competition and an open discussion forum are good for that. But I may hold back technology that I believe will benefit others more than her.

Telemetry is an invaluable source of insight. I couldn't find an existing tool that met my needs, and with my background, skill set, and the rise of AI agents, it became viable to just write it myself.

If you do have access to this feature, my conditions are:

Open data

In racing, data is traditionally a closely guarded secret used to maintain a competitive edge. However, if the goal is to grow, to be faster & better than yesterday's self, to be part of something bigger (both in space and across time), then sharing knowledge becomes a better strategy.

For this reason, I want athletes to be able to compare laps and learn from one another. And for the time being, any laps uploaded to the site will be visible to other users who have telemetry access. You don't have to upload all your data; the rFactor 2 plugin allows per-kart configuration (for example, configuring only kart 8 to send telemetry). But if you want to see the telemetry — others will be able to see it too. And you will be able to see their data.

These sharing policies will likely evolve over time (e.g., introducing publication delays or allowing you to keep some laps private), but the guiding principle will remain: contribute data to access data, even if not strictly 1:1. The goal is to keep fast, representative laps open for everyone to learn from.

Reliability and project longevity

I cannot guarantee high availability or long-term reliability. Things may break while I am away or busy, or I simply may lose interest or decide that it's time to move on to a new project. However:

  • Reviewing telemetry delivers value here and now. If the service is interrupted or data is lost, that would be unfortunate, but the insights and driving gains you achieved along the way remain with you.
  • You can get your data and analyze it through different tools. Modern coding agents, such as Antigravity, should have no problem converting it to the needed format.
  • The project is open source on GitHub. The codebase is complete, so anyone can spin up an independent instance if my server goes away, though note that track configuration data and installation instructions are not provided — running your own instance is possible, but requires some effort.

It is free

My employer has blanket approval for personal open-source, non-profit projects, so absorbing running costs (which at this point are very minimal) is the easiest option for me.
